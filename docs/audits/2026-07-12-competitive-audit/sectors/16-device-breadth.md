# S16 · Device & firmware breadth

Products rated: KerfDesk, LightBurn 2.1.03 (Core $99 / Pro $199), MillMage 0.8.02 (CNC-only — but S16 is device & firmware breadth and the market pack supplies full MillMage device evidence, and its vendor states it "competes fully in this device sector", so it is rated here, not N/R), LaserGRBL 7.14.1 (dormant ~16 months), XCS (= xTool Studio desktop V1.7.x per phase-0 roster note; the discontinued XCS's successor), Easel (web SaaS, Free/Starter/Pro), gSender (Sienci Labs, free).
N/R: none — every roster product connects to and drives machines, so all compete on firmware/controller breadth, profile catalogs and setup onboarding. No cell is `U`: documentary or code evidence was found for all 35.
Pinned evidence: KerfDesk @ e0a10b6c (worktree HEAD at rating time; evidence pack `data/S16-evidence.json`; the load-bearing CODE claims — 6-kind `ControllerKind` union, FluidNC driver's missing console-write guard (A.3.9), and the 12-entry `GRBL_MACHINE_PROFILE_CATALOG` — were re-read and confirmed at rating time. ### State of play

Device & firmware breadth is a sector where KerfDesk is competitive but never leads — it sits mid-pack on the two categories that reward breadth and near-parity on the two that reward depth of *design*, while conceding the one category (DSP) that defines the high-margin CO2 laser market outright. The shape of the roster is worth stating first: LightBurn is the reference in all five categories (four 9s and the sector's only 10), MillMage — LightBurn's own CNC sibling — trails it by a point almost everywhere, and the rest of the field splits sharply between "open, multi-firmware" tools (KerfDesk, gSender, LaserGRBL) and "closed, single-vendor" tools (XCS) with Easel straddling. KerfDesk's honest position is "as broad as the free/open tools on paper, deeper in code, but unproven on hardware and missing the DSP tier LightBurn Pro owns."

On GRBL-family depth (16.1) KerfDesk is genuinely strong at the code level: a pure `$$` state-machine parser that harvests the whole settings block, decoded alarm/error tables, native `$J` jog, a G38.2 probe, and — since CTL-01 — capability-gated override bytes. Where it stops short of LightBurn (9) is at the edges that the sector actually measures: grblHAL is a four-line driver delta (its errors 39+ fall through to raw text) and FluidNC is a read-only-dump delta whose console *still accepts* numeric `$N=value` writes because the driver ships no `prepareConsoleCommand` guard — a discrepancy sharp enough that the catalog note at `profile-catalog.ts:237` advertises "blocked in-app" while the driver does not actually block the raw path (A.3.9, confirmed by re-read this audit). That un-ADR'd divergence from safe handling is why KerfDesk lands at 7, tied with gSender (grbl + grblHAL first-class, community-trusted) and a point above the pure-grbl LaserGRBL and Easel (6). XCS scores 0 here — not out of weakness but by design: it exposes no generic grbl controller path at all.

Other firmware (16.2) is KerfDesk's best relative showing and the source of its cleanest win. The tree implements Marlin (ADR-095) and Smoothieware (ADR-096) as real drivers with dialect-specific behavior — M400 settle instead of a hardcoded dwell, queued M114 status polled only while idle, a comma-status fallback parser, fractional-S rescaling — which is more than any competitor except LightBurn (8, the mature reference) offers. MillMage covers Smoothieware (beta) + Duet/RepRap over HTTP plus a wide CAM post-export list but no Marlin (6); LaserGRBL has experimental/early-stage Smoothie+Marlin (3); Easel manages only a token OEM-partner G-code export path (2); and gSender and XCS have nothing off the grbl family (0). So KerfDesk's Marlin+Smoothieware support is a real capability the entire free-sender field lacks — with the standing caveat that it is **simulator-verified only** and carries the open A.3.3 streamingMode-mismatch path.

DSP/proprietary controllers (16.3) is the sector's decisive loss. LightBurn Pro drives Ruida, Trocen, TopWisdom and galvo systems — a 10, unmatched — and everyone else is at 0 except KerfDesk's token 2. That 2 is not a strength: it is an experimental, file-only `.rd` export from a clean-room encoder that "NO file has been accepted by a real Ruida controller yet," has no live transport (no socket in the tree), and self-labels EXPERIMENTAL on every save. It edges the other competitors only because they offer literally nothing; it does not approach usable DSP support, and the honest read is that KerfDesk cannot serve a CO2/DSP buyer today. ADR-097 records the experimental scope as a deliberate limit, so this is a recorded decision rather than an un-flagged hole — but it is still the single largest per-category delta in the sector (−8 vs LightBurn).

Profile catalog (16.4) and setup wizard (16.5) are the "solid, not leading" middle. KerfDesk ships 12 machine profiles, each with a typed evidence badge (most public-spec/simulator-tested, one HW-claimed), and pairs them with a start-gate that cross-checks a profile's declared bed and feed against the live `$130/$131`/`$110/$111` the controller reports — a correctness guard the evidence fairly calls stronger than LightBurn's. But 12 profiles with no RESEARCH_LOG provenance, a guessed `.lbdev` import schema, and no app-level saved-machines list cannot match LightBurn's Find-My-Laser auto-identify + importable manufacturer bundles + broad list (9) or MillMage's searchable machine database (8); KerfDesk (6) ties the single-vendor XCS and Easel and edges gSender (5) and LaserGRBL (2, no shipped catalog). The wizard tells the same story: KerfDesk's 7-step draft-commit flow, prefilled from the live `$$` dump with guarded verify-by-reread firmware writes and exhaustive `assertNever` state handling, is arguably the most sophisticated *design* in the roster and carries a real differentiator ("your laser told us its settings") — but it is held at 7 by two open defects (A.3.3, A.4.3) and by never having been exercised at runtime or on hardware this audit, below LightBurn's mature two-path reference (9) and MillMage (8), level with XCS/Easel/gSender.

### Not verified

- **No §5.3 fidelity cells and no §5.4 `⚠HW` cells in this sector** — every score is a plain capability rating; no PERC evidence was required and no `*` design-intent stars were assigned.
- **16.1** — No real-hardware grblHAL or FluidNC behavior. The FluidNC "simulator" is the GRBL sim with a FluidNC banner; whether a real FluidNC `$$` dump even includes `$30/$32` (which the power-scale gate depends on) is unproven. The grblHAL "hardware-verified" claim (Falcon A1 Pro, `profile-catalog.ts:215-217`) was taken from catalog evidence text, **CLAIMED per the repo ledger, not re-verified**. The A.3.9 missing-console-guard was code-confirmed this audit; its real-controller effect was not.
- **16.2** — All real-hardware Marlin/Smoothieware behavior: both are **simulator-verified only** per ADR-095/096 ("NOT hardware-verified"). Whether stock Smoothie emits comma vs pipe status is unconfirmed (the fix is a tolerant parser, not a board check); a real Marlin's reaction to a stray override byte is unconfirmed; the A.3.3 streamingMode-mismatch is a designed-open path, not exercised on hardware.
- **16.3** — No real Ruida controller has accepted KerfDesk output (labeled experimental everywhere). No LightBurn/MeerK40t reference `.rd` exists in the tree to diff against — the golden test proves byte-stability across code changes, not protocol correctness. What a real Ruida DSP does with Min==Max power is unverified; UDP transport is deliberately not built.
- **16.4** — Vendor-published bed dimensions for xTool D1 Pro / Sculpfun S30 / Ortur LM3 / Falcon / Neotronics were not verified (no network access). The real LightBurn `.lbdev` file format is unknown (no genuine sample; the importer parses a guessed schema). Whether `.lfmachine.json` still drops `baudRate`/`cameraAlignment` (A.4.8) was not re-checked. The Falcon "hardware-verified" claim is from evidence text.
- **16.5** — Hardware-gated per ADR-092: that the wizard reads `$$` correctly on a real GRBL connect, that writing `$30/$32` verifies via re-read, and that the chosen origin corner flips Y correctly in emitted G-code are all unverified.
