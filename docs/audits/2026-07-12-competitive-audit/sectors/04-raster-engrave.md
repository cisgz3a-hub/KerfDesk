### State of play

This is the sector the LightBurn-switcher persona weights highest of all eighteen (10/100), and it is also the sector where LightBurn's lead over KerfDesk is widest and best-evidenced. LightBurn's image pipeline is the community-acknowledged photo-engraving reference: the roster's superset of dither modes (including the Halftone/Newsprint angle-screen family nobody else has), grayscale power mapping community-rated excellent for depth work, arbitrary scan angles with per-pass increments, dot-width correction, per-layer overscan, and a true pass-through for pre-dithered sources. Its sector mean here is ~9.0 against KerfDesk's ~5.9 — the only KerfDesk row within one point of the leader is line interval / DPI control.

KerfDesk's raster core is real and structurally well-proven: eleven dither modes (a near-superset of LightBurn's error-diffusion family — eight kernels to LightBurn's five or so — but missing halftone/newsprint entirely), a grayscale mode with the same Min/Max power model as LightBurn's, correct M4 dynamic-power emission with modal S and run-length compression, and 43 dither tests plus the full raster suite re-run green this session. But the §5.3 fidelity cap bites hard and correctly here: there is zero perceptual coverage of any dither or grayscale output anywhere in the repo — the perceptual harness covers trace only — and the grayscale hardware burn is still pending (F.2.f). Both fidelity cells sit at the capped 6, and honestly so: `dither.ts` itself documents that most diode lasers do not respond linearly to S and that no gamma/power LUT compensates, which is precisely the kind of defect only a rendered comparison or a burn would expose.

Where KerfDesk genuinely stands tall is the control-plane engineering around the pipeline. The Adjust Image dialog (brightness/contrast/gamma/invert with source-vs-processed preview and persisted user presets), closed-vector image masking with crop-to-mask and processed-bitmap export, matches most of LightBurn's documented adjustment surface and adds presets LightBurn's docs don't mention. The paired Line Interval + DPI fields with a test-proven 5–25 lines/mm clamp, a warn threshold, and a pre-compile pixel/memory budget are guardrails the prior audit confirmed LightBurn lacks — the strongest KerfDesk row in the sector at 8. ADR-052's speed-interpolated scan-offset compensation with a calibration-pattern generator is a sophisticated touch aimed squarely at the bidirectional-zipper problem, though its effectiveness is hardware-dependent and unburned.

The losses are concentrated and specific. Raster transform freedom (4.5) carries the sector's worst user-facing wall: a rotated image is refused at preflight ("clear rotation before engraving") while LightBurn engraves rotated images — the prior audit's S9-F2 MAJOR, confirmed unchanged — and even xTool Studio applies full canvas rotation to bitmaps. Scan options (4.6) has no scan angle for images at all and a fixed 5 mm overscan compile constant where LightBurn offers per-layer control. And pass-through (4.7) is subtly broken as shipped: KerfDesk still runs the layer's dither over pass-through pixels, so a user importing a pre-dithered image — the mode's entire purpose — gets it re-dithered, while the UI tooltip claims processing is skipped. No ADR records this divergence, which per §5.6 classifies it as a bug, not a choice.

Among the rest of the roster, LaserGRBL remains the credible free alternative: community-verified dither breadth, a documented S-MIN/S-MAX grayscale tool rated close to LightBurn for basic photos (7, above KerfDesk's capped 6 — though that comparison is cap-artifact as much as capability), but import-time-only adjustments, no on-canvas object model, and an unknown pass-through/negative story. xTool Studio covers the basics across every row — seven dither modes, default grayscale, rich adjustment sliders, full object transforms — but exposes almost no expert control (no power-curve control, no gamma, metric-only interval, angle gated to one machine's vector mode) and has essentially no community quality signal yet. MillMage and Easel do not compete: both are CNC products with no laser raster pipeline, rated N/R throughout.
