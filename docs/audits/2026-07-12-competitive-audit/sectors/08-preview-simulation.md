### State of play

Preview is a sector KerfDesk takes unusually seriously at the architecture level, and it shows in the one place that matters most: geometric fidelity (8.1). ADR-040's single prepared-output pipeline means Preview, Save, Start, Estimate, and .rd export all consume the same compiled job, a parity test locks the preview deep-equal to what Save/Start emit, and — uniquely in this audit — perceptual ink-mask tests re-run green this session diff both the preview toolpath and the emitted G-code against source-geometry truth masks. That is a stronger *verifiable* fidelity guarantee than any competitor offers: every rival cell in 8.1 is a vendor claim, and Carbide Create's community actively reports blank and missing renders. KerfDesk's 7 sits below LightBurn's 9 only because the perceptual scope is narrow (32 px synthetic fill/cross-hatch rectangles; line-mode, raster alignment, rotary, and the actual on-canvas pixels are unproven) while LightBurn's preview is community-treated ground truth at production scale.

Everything interactive around that solid core is a step behind. Playback (8.2) has the full control surface — scrubber, play/pause, three speeds, travel toggle, a CNC pass stepper, a 3D dialog at the scrubber, and even external .nc programs playable in the same simulator — but the animation is distance-compressed over fixed 60/30/10-second wall durations rather than time-based, so a slow engrave and a rapid travel animate at the same rate (full-sweep S4-F3, confirmed unchanged). LightBurn, MillMage, and xTool Studio all animate at documented multiples of *actual* speed (1/10×–40×), which is the difference between a cut-order viewer and a genuine simulation; LightBurn's Start-Here-resume-from-the-slider remains the reference. LaserGRBL and gSender have no pre-run playback at all — a real KerfDesk win over both, and over Carbide Create's scrub-less animation.

Time estimation (8.3) is the sector's most honest illustration of the model-vs-measurement gap. KerfDesk ships the roster's most physically serious pre-run model — a clean-room grbl planner with junction-deviation cornering, two-pass lookahead, and trapezoidal block times, with the prior exit-velocity underestimate (M-30) verifiably fixed — yet not one timing has ever been taken against a real machine, and three known defects remain (raster ETA ignores the unidirectional setting, the live estimate ignores job placement, and there is no elapsed/remaining display while streaming). Meanwhile the market data shows every incumbent's estimate is only as good as its calibration story: LightBurn drifts badly until synced to the controller, VCarve requires user scale factors of up to 3–4×, Carbide Create runs 30–40% under, and LaserGRBL openly ships a crude pre-run number refined by a very accurate in-run projection — a two-tier design KerfDesk lacks entirely. A physics model with zero validation cannot honestly outscore community-calibrated incumbents, hence 5.

Power/mode visualization (8.4) is the sector's clearest un-ADR'd LightBurn divergence. KerfDesk's raster preview is genuinely WYSIWYG — it renders the compile path's own dither and its cache key tracks the emitted S range — but every vector cut strokes in one fixed theme blue because `drawCut` ignores its layer-color parameter: 10% and 90% power are visually identical, there is no shade-by-power and no toggle, and ADR-028 itself records that LightBurn's preview "shades according to power." Per §5.6 that is a bug, not a choice. On the CNC side (8.5) KerfDesk lands surprisingly well: a genuine scrub-coupled removal grid whose V-carve depth math is perceptually verified against an analytic groove, plus a re-import parity test proving its own emitted .nc reproduces the native removal footprint — instrumentation neither MillMage nor Carbide Create can show. VCarve's textured, animated, both-sides preview remains the untouchable ceiling, and KerfDesk's grid is a 2.5D heightfield on a flat rectangle whose three.js rendering has never actually been looked at.

Performance (8.6) is defensively engineered — segment budgets, stride sampling with an explicit banner, cancellable rebuilds, and two previously-found hot loops verifiably closed — but never measured at production scale, and four of eight competitor cells are U for the same reason (no data exists). gSender is the only product with a documented, user-facing degradation strategy (Lightweight Mode's three presets), and LightBurn earns its 8 on community silence about scaling problems despite routine multi-hour raster previews. The roster splits cleanly: LightBurn owns the laser-preview crown (9/10/8/10 across its four rated laser cells), VCarve owns CNC simulation, and KerfDesk is the only product competing credibly on *both* sides of the laser/CNC line — third-best overall in the sector, with its losses concentrated in playback realism, estimate validation, and vector power shading rather than in architecture.
