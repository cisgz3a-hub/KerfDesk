Products rated: KerfDesk, LightBurn 2.1.03 (Core $99 / Pro $199), MillMage 0.8.02 (CNC-only — machine control/streaming is not laser-only, so it competes here), LaserGRBL 7.14.1 (dormant ~16 months), XCS (= xTool Studio desktop V1.7.30 per the phase-0 roster note), Easel (web SaaS, Free/Starter/Pro), gSender 1.6.2 (Tier-2, rated in S10 per design §3).

### State of play

Machine control & streaming is one of KerfDesk's weakest competitive sectors, and the honest read is stark: **KerfDesk wins zero categories and trails the roster leader in all eight.** This is the surface where hardware-adjacent maturity dominates — years of hardening across a real user base — and a young MIT web PWA has not caught up to two mature senders (gSender, LightBurn), a same-vendor CNC sibling (MillMage), or a vertically-integrated consumer app (XCS). KerfDesk is genuinely competitive on connect UX, status surfaces, and streaming *design* (all 7), but clearly behind on jog, framing, fire, macros, and multi-device management.

The roster's centre of gravity is **gSender**, which is the sector reference three times over: best-in-class jog (10 — continuous jog, 70+ shortcuts, speed presets, six go-to workspaces), the richest console/macros (9) and status surface (9 — 3-axis DRO, plain-English alarm Helper, EEPROM editor), and the community-proven streaming/recovery leader (9*). LightBurn and MillMage form a mature-parity tier (mostly 7–8) sharing one device architecture; LaserGRBL is the dormant free baseline (mostly 3–6); XCS scores well exactly where closed vertical integration helps (auto-connect 7, framing 7, on-device execution 6*) and collapses where it doesn't (no console/macros, no DRO or firmware editor).

KerfDesk's **connect UX (7)** is its most competitive cell: WebSerial with discriminated-union `connecting/connected/failed/disconnected` states, advisory welcome-banner auto-detect that surfaces a mismatch but never silently switches drivers, a profile catalog with suggest-and-apply, a 6-step device-setup wizard, and a wrong-baud hint routed into the console (S6-F1). What keeps it a point behind LightBurn and gSender (8): WebSerial forces the user to pick the port from the browser dialog (no true auto-select), the flow is browser-gated with a documented Brave/Shields caveat, and it models a single active device. Good ergonomics, near-parity, but not seamless.

The **jog gap (10.2, score 4)** is the sector's most visible deficit. KerfDesk offers step-jog only — nine XY steps and a focus/Z jog — on a hardcoded feed with *no speed selector*, *no continuous/hold-down jog*, *no arrow-key jog* (only Ctrl+Return/Ctrl+. start/stop), and *no go-to-origin/park button*. This is the full LightBurn Move-window parity gap (S6-F3). KerfDesk's origin-aware direction and its unique no-go-zone jog refusal (DEV-04 — no competitor guards a jog against keep-out zones) are real merits, but they do not lift the category: a user who wants to hold-jog into position, vary jog speed, or return to origin hits a wall immediately. Notably, even LaserGRBL and Easel (both 5) edge KerfDesk here purely because they expose a jog-speed control it lacks entirely.

**Fire (10.4)** is absent and, worse, un-recorded. There is no `firePulse`/`testPulse` anywhere in the tree and no ADR that adds or deliberately excludes a Fire button — an un-recorded divergence from LightBurn on exactly the Creality Falcon diode machines the shipped catalog targets, precisely the class where LightBurn users rely on Fire for focus and alignment. Per design §5.6 this classifies as bug-severity, not a design choice. Framing is only a partial substitute (a beam-off box; it cannot show "where is the spot now"). LightBurn's dedicated Fire button (9) is the roster standard; XCS's built-in laser-spot mode (6) and LaserGRBL's low-power macro (3) fill the same need in their own idioms; the three CNC-only products are N/R.

Where KerfDesk holds its own is **status surfaces (10.6, 7 — tying LightBurn and MillMage)** and **streaming design (10.7, 7*)**. Its alarm decoding is a genuine strength: GRBL 1.1 alarms 1–9 plus grblHAL 10–13, each with title/detail/position-lost flag and a recovery action driving a gated red banner — that rivals gSender's Helper and *exceeds* LightBurn's un-decoded console alarms. But it ships only guided key-setting writes ($30/$32), not the full editable `$$` grid that LightBurn, MillMage, LaserGRBL, Easel, and gSender all provide, which is why the category sits at parity rather than ahead. And the streamer is, on paper, the most defensively engineered in the roster — char-counted with ping-pong fallback, oversized-line refusal, terminal-ack absorption (trailing `ok`s after `error:N` can't fake a clean finish), an untracked-ack ledger, a stall watchdog, and a lone-M0 tool-change hold — yet it carries the entire proof-debt: real serial throughput, RX-buffer headroom on a genuine 128-byte controller, and multi-hour stability are all CLAIMED, never run on hardware. So it ties battle-tested-but-limited LightBurn (7*) and trails community-proven gSender (9*). Multi-device management (10.8, 2 — a deliberate, ADR-recorded single-device deferral) and console macros (10.5, 5 — a strong console with zero user macros, also a recorded deferral) round out the deficits.

### Where we win / where we lose

- **No category wins in S10.** KerfDesk does not lead or tie for the lead in any of the eight categories; its ceiling is 7 and the best competitor is ≥ 8 in every category (so §5.5 flags nothing to refute). Its genuine relative strengths — the DEV-04 no-go-zone jog guard, GRBL/grblHAL alarm decoding with recovery actions, the safe-Z framing retract, and the defensively-engineered streamer — are all real but each sits inside a category KerfDesk still loses, so none anchors a win bullet.
- LOSE 10.4 (−9 vs LightBurn) — no Fire/test-pulse control exists at all and no ADR records the omission, on exactly the Falcon diode machines the catalog targets. → G-S10-1
- LOSE 10.2 (−6 vs gSender, −4 vs LightBurn/MillMage) — step-jog on a fixed feed; no continuous, arrow-key, speed, or go-to/park. → G-S10-2
- LOSE 10.8 (−6 vs LightBurn/MillMage/gSender) — single active device, no saved-machine roster or side-by-side switching (ADR-recorded deferral). → G-S10-3
- LOSE 10.5 (−4 vs gSender, −3 vs LightBurn/MillMage) — a strong console but zero user-definable macros (doc-recorded deferral). → G-S10-4
- LOSE 10.3 (−4 vs LightBurn) — bounding-box framing only; no rubber-band (min-perimeter) frame and no low-power beam-on trace-outline. → G-S10-5
- LOSE 10.6 (−2 vs gSender) — no full labeled `$$` firmware-settings editor grid (guided key writes only); alarm decode keeps it at parity with LightBurn. → G-S10-6
- LOSE 10.7 (−2 vs gSender) — streamer robustness proven only structurally (tests + pure reducer + simulator), zero hardware/long-job proof, vs gSender's community-proven stability. → G-S10-7

### Not verified
