### State of play

Image trace is one of KerfDesk's most deliberately engineered surfaces: a clean-room contour engine (ADR-123) upgraded by the ADR-128 measured-boundary pipeline (sub-pixel iso-line extraction, 2× supersampling, Whittaker–Henderson fair-then-fit cubic fitting), a from-scratch 24-module medial-axis centerline lane, and an ADR-030 dialog modeled directly on LightBurn's. Critically for the §5.3 fidelity cap, the perceptual evidence is real and was re-run during this audit: the 1024×1024 real-logo baseline hits IoU 0.953 with zero open polylines and a worst traced-vertex 1.41px from drawn ink, the centerline bar holds every ground-truth stroke under 0.79px deviation, and the six-benchmark rated loop audits at 10/10. ADR-128 also records a maintainer-approved 15-iteration visual reference-loop pass. That is more perceptual proof than any competitor cell in this sector carries — but it is proof in isolation, not comparison.

That distinction drives the 3.1 numbers. LightBurn's Potrace-licensed Trace Image is the community-acknowledged reference in this roster: clean on high-contrast logos and line art, with a known fine-detail weakness that users work around by upscaling input. KerfDesk's engine is explicitly engineered past classic Potrace weaknesses (auto-upscale of small/thin sources, sub-pixel boundary measurement, crack-filling), and its measured numbers are strong — but no side-by-side against LightBurn's output exists anywhere (ADR-123/ADR-030 record that as outstanding, and KerfDesk's own perceptual test header conservatively states "NOT as good as LightBurn"). Under the no-optimism rule KerfDesk takes 7 to LightBurn's 8: daily-drivable, perceptually proven, parity unproven. MillMage documents a control-for-control copy of LightBurn's dialog but has zero community quality data — the shared-engine assumption is [INF] and its 6 should be treated as soft.

Centerline (3.2) is the sector's clearest structural difference. LightBurn 2.1 simply does not have one — a years-open feature request with users routed to Inkscape — and Easel likewise offers outline-only tracing. LaserGRBL documents a centerline option whose own manual concedes lower accuracy; xTool Studio documents a "Center tracing" mode with no quality evidence at all. KerfDesk's medial-axis lane, with re-run sub-pixel perceptual bars, is the best-evidenced centerline in the roster and takes the sector-leading 8. It stops short of 9–10 because the perceptual proof is synthetic ground-truth strokes (bars, corners, crosses, arcs) plus benchmark fixtures — no real handwriting, signature, or complex sketch has been perceptually verified, and ADR-058's corner/arc thinning polish is still pinned as pending.

On controls (3.3) KerfDesk implements the LightBurn model faithfully — Cutoff/Threshold band, Ignore Less Than, Smoothness, Optimize, alpha mask, sketch trace, drag-boundary crop/enhance — and goes beyond it with five tuned presets (LightBurn documents none) and a live preview that is architecturally honest: it runs the exact commit trace function on the exact 2048px commit pixel grid, so what you preview is what you get. But three in-tree defects keep it a point behind the reference: every control is a bare number input where LightBurn ships draggable sliders (S9-F9), there is no keyboard shortcut, and — worst — the default Line Art preset's auto-sketch branch silently disables the user's Cutoff/Threshold settings with no off switch (S9-F1), an un-ADR'd behavioral divergence that per §5.6 classifies as a bug.

Photo and multi-tone handling (3.4) is where the roster's only documented capability lead against KerfDesk appears. KerfDesk (like LightBurn and MillMage) deliberately routes photographs to raster Image Mode and handles non-binary sources through auto-sketch local-contrast promotion, an Edge Detection lane sharing the ADR-128 finisher, adjustments, and ADR-113 region-enhance — all with perceptual coverage re-run this session. But no multi-tone or multi-colour vectorization is reachable from the UI (the imagetracerjs lane is dead code), while xTool Studio documents color-block auto-tracing with a "Layer by Color" switch that auto-splits traced vectors into per-color layers — the strongest documented multi-tone story here, though its quality is unverified ([OFF] existence only). The S9-F1 auto-sketch trap also lands precisely on colour-rich sources. Robustness (3.5) is a near-vacuum on the competitor side — four of five competitor cells are U — so KerfDesk's 7 (measured 2.2s at cap, decode-bomb guards, worker fallback, fail-closed parsing, supersample budget caps) rests on its own evidence, and the category is LOW-CONFIDENCE for cross-roster conclusions.

For personas, this sector weighs 7/100 for the LightBurn-switcher laser hobbyist, 2/100 for the CNC beginner, and 4/100 for the production shop: the centerline win and the LightBurn-model dialog matter most to the first persona, which is exactly where the S9-F1 silent-override defect is most likely to be noticed.
