### State of play

This is a strong sector for KerfDesk on paper: it out-designs the roster on preflight (11.5), matches or exceeds LightBurn on safety posture (11.8) and run-selection (11.7), and ships the roster's only *automatic* crash-checkpoint recovery (11.4). But two structural facts hold the sector honest. First, the two categories a buyer cares about most under failure — pause/resume correctness and power-loss recovery — are §5.4 hardware-dependent, and every KerfDesk claim there is `[HW-CLAIMED]`: test-proven in code, never driven on a machine. Second, gSender, the roster's field-proven recovery/sender reference, genuinely leads two of the categories KerfDesk would like to own (11.4 recovery, 11.6 in-run feedback). The result is a sector where KerfDesk's *engineering* is arguably the deepest, but its *proof* is the thinnest, and one specific, confirmed miss (no mid-job ETA) is visible to any user on the very first run.

The clearest genuine lead is preflight (11.5). KerfDesk runs a deep pre-write gate — parameter-range checks, layer-mode mismatch, open-contour detection for offset/island fills, unsupported-raster-transform, machine-profile and bed-bounds, no-go zones, laser-on-travel, and a non-finite (NaN/Infinity) coordinate guard — and, uniquely, proves the controller's `$30`/`$32` settings against live firmware before it emits a byte. Because KerfDesk is CAM *and* sender, that gate spans the whole pipeline; gSender's well-regarded preflight (on-load G-code warnings, a Soft-Limits work-area warning, an Outline trace) is strong but operates only on already-compiled G-code, and LightBurn's is framing plus out-of-bounds warnings. The 9 (not 10) is held down by the open M-17 defect — the default CNC layer is a full-depth through-cut with holding tabs off, and cnc-preflight issues no warning — plus a latent gap (arc-bulge past the bed is not caught by the text bounds scanner) and the fact that physical bed-fit is unverified.

Recovery (11.4) is the sector's headline story and its sharpest honesty test. KerfDesk's fingerprint-gated checkpoint (schema v3: FNV-1a fingerprint, sendable/acked line counts, machine kind, output scope, resolved job origin) recompiles the project on resume and refuses on any byte difference — a genuinely more *automatic* design than the manual approaches around it. LightBurn and MillMage have no automatic recovery at all, only a manual Preview 'Start Here' time-slider scrub; LaserGRBL, XCS and Easel have nothing — community consensus for LaserGRBL is that continuing an interrupted job is simply impossible, and Easel's official guidance is to re-home, re-zero and re-carve. Those are 0s (absent for a product that competes), not `N/R`. But gSender's 'Start from Line' is purpose-built for exactly this — power-loss, USB disconnect, overnight recovery — replays accessories/spindle/laser power/macros, and is field-proven, earning the category's 9*. KerfDesk's 8* trails it by one because the automatic design is unverified on hardware and carries a real residual defect (a single global checkpoint slot: starting another job overwrites an unresumed record). So KerfDesk decisively beats the *rest* of the field on recovery while sitting just behind the one competitor built around it.

Safety posture (11.8) splits on a hardware line. KerfDesk has the deepest *software* posture in the roster: laser-off-on-travel enforced three ways, the `$30`/`$32` readiness gate, pause refused on a laser without proven `$32=1`, six runtime safety-notice kinds that name the physical e-stop, a watchdog for silent USB death, GRBL Door-state/interlock awareness, and fire/eye/fumes/PVC guidance in Help and the install EULA. That is materially more mechanism than LightBurn's framing-plus-docs. But XCS wins the category outright at 9 because xTool controls the hardware and can therefore *enforce* what everyone else can only advise — flame/spark detection, tilt/move stop, enclosure interlock, and LSO safety-training gating. KerfDesk, like LightBurn/gSender/MillMage, drives third-party machines and cannot enforce an interlock; its 8 also carries two honest dents — the safety notice is on-demand (Help) plus EULA rather than a mandatory first-run gate, and the M-17 beginner-hostile default undercuts the posture.

Run-selection (11.7) is a quiet strength: KerfDesk carries *both* halves of the capability — object-level cut-selected with a selection-origin option (LightBurn parity) and start-from-line modal-state replay (gSender-style) — and freezes the selective scope into the checkpoint so a partial burn can resume. LightBurn leads at 9 because its equivalent pair is field-proven and its Preview 'Start Here' is more mature; but gSender, MillMage and XCS each have only one half (run-from-line, or an output toggle), so KerfDesk's completeness is a real +2 over them. Start-position modes (11.1) and stop/abort (11.3) round out a competent middle: LightBurn's Absolute/Current/User Origin model with a 9-dot anchor selector remains the roster's most complete positioning surface (9), and KerfDesk's four-mode union plus a verified-frame bounds check is close behind at 8 — dragged off parity by a confirmed friction defect, jobPlacement is not persisted to `.lf2` and resets to absolute/front-left every session.

The one place KerfDesk visibly loses to almost everyone is in-run feedback (11.6). Its alarm surfacing is arguably the best in the roster — GRBL 1-9 and grblHAL 10-13 humanized with title, detail and a recovery action in a red banner — but there is *no mid-job elapsed or remaining ETA*: the progress bar is lines-and-percent only, the pre-job estimate does not update during the run, and the WORKFLOW F-B11 Phase-C label was never built. gSender (9), LaserGRBL, LightBurn and MillMage (all 7) show live remaining time; a user watching a long engrave sees KerfDesk fall a clear step behind here, and it is the sector's cleanest gap.
