### State of play

Camera & registration is a laser-app sector, and the numbers split accordingly: the two mature laser tools (LightBurn, XCS) own it, KerfDesk is solid-but-trailing, and the CNC/free tools barely register. LightBurn and XCS post the highest sector means (both ~7.6–7.8); KerfDesk lands ~5.8, dragged down by exactly one absence; MillMage scores only through a probing analog (~1.0); LaserGRBL and Easel are effectively empty. A roster note up front: MillMage and Easel are **rated, not N/R**, because S12 is broader than "camera" — it includes registration and fixturing, where a CNC router genuinely competes (probing, work offsets, manual zero). Per design §5.1 their lack of a camera is therefore a scored **0**, not a "does-not-compete."

Calibration (12.1) is the surprise near-parity. KerfDesk's suite is objectively rich — a printable checkerboard with live corner detection, a fisheye/Brown-Conrady LM solve gated by pose-diversity and trust with an A/B review step, and a bed-alignment wizard that **burns a 5-marker pattern and auto-detects it with no clicks** — plus a manual 4-point bed-corner fallback. Structurally that matches LightBurn's two-stage model (lens distortion then workspace alignment) and adds more automation than LightBurn's engraved-target manual alignment. But it is code-and-tests only; nothing here has been proven against a real camera, and no camera perceptual fixture exists in the tree. LightBurn earns 9 for a mature, proven, preset-backed workflow (finicky but battle-tested); XCS earns 8 for camera-first guided per-machine calibrations that are a core onboarding step; KerfDesk earns 8 on capability breadth with the proof caveat carrying the one-point gap to LightBurn.

Overlay accuracy (12.2) is the sector's `*` hardware category, so every cell is a design-intent rating. XCS leads at 9 — camera-first WYSIWYG placement is its signature strength, with community-documented ~0.3 mm on the P2's dual-camera Aimed Measure. LightBurn's proven live overlay with Width/Height/X-Y-Shift nudge and 2.1 multi/IP-camera support sits at 8. KerfDesk lands at 6: the overlay implementation is genuinely complete — a composed `matrix3d` of homography × view × CSS scale, opacity, live/still paths, and the two 2026-07-10 mis-registration bugs (apply-time resolution rescale and basis honoring, now shared with Trace via one helper so they cannot diverge) both fixed — but its accuracy is entirely HW-CLAIMED (ADR-107/134 say so in as many words), and a rectified-basis alignment on a live USB stream is deliberately replaced by a "capture a still" notice rather than a per-frame live de-fisheye. The feature exists and is well-architected; what's missing is proof, not code.

Trace-from-camera (12.3) is a genuine KerfDesk strength that still trails on maturity. ADR-110 warps the aligned frame top-down into bed-millimetre space through the inverse homography and hands it to the trace pipeline as a `RasterImage` whose bounds *are* the bed, so traced vectors land at true machine coordinates with no manual positioning — a registration nicety LightBurn's plain camera-trace does not advertise. But at `TRACE_PIXELS_PER_MM = 4` (~102 dpi) it is outline-oriented, and its output fidelity and physical landing were never perceptually or hardware verified. LightBurn's camera capture-to-editable-vectors and XCS's camera auto-trace/centerline are both mature, documented features and take 8; KerfDesk takes 7.

Print-and-cut (12.4) is the dominant loss and the reason KerfDesk's sector mean sits below the laser leaders. KerfDesk has **no** print-and-cut / registration-mark workflow: ADR-109 explicitly lists it "Out of scope … the natural v3.5 follow-on," a repo-wide grep for print-and-cut/registration/fiducial/crop-mark returns zero implementation hits, and the only adjacent capability — markerless trace-from-camera — is a different feature that does not register a cut to an *externally printed* sheet. LightBurn's two-target Print-and-Cut is the roster reference (8); XCS has a machine/module-dependent "print & blade cut" plus camera positioning onto pre-printed media (6); KerfDesk is a flat 0. Because this absence is ADR-recorded, §5.6 does not bump its severity — it is a deliberate deferral, not an un-ADR'd divergence — but it is still a headline parity gap for any buyer who prints-then-cuts.

Fixture & board capture (12.5) is where KerfDesk clearly leads. It is the only roster product with a **dedicated board-capture tool**: jog to a physical board's corners, set a G92 origin, draw an exact outline, then centre / Fit / Array artwork across it (ADR-124/125/126), circle boards included, with order-independent geometry, off-square diagnostics, and provenance-locking — alongside the older ADR-057 registration jig. LightBurn's own docs concede it has "no dedicated fixture library" and repurposes print-and-cut marks plus camera-overlay eyeball placement (6); MillMage offers reliable but bare probe-origin-plus-work-offset repeatability (5); XCS's camera Batch/Smart Fill is a polished camera-driven batch placement (7); Easel and LaserGRBL have nothing dedicated (0). KerfDesk takes 8. The honest asymmetry: KerfDesk leads on dedicated *tooling breadth*, code-and-tests proven, while its actual G92/jog **landing** accuracy is HW-CLAIMED, whereas LightBurn's camera repeat and MillMage's probing are hardware-proven methods. The lead is real at the capability layer; it is not a proven-accuracy lead.

- (within noise) 12.1 (−1 vs LightBurn) and 12.3 (−1 vs LightBurn/XCS) — capability is at or near parity; the residual gap is proof and maturity, not missing features. Not registered as gaps per design §11 (±1 = noise).

### Gaps feeding the register

- **G-S12-1 · 12.4 Print-and-cut / registration marks · vs LightBurn (−8), XCS (−6)** · ADR-recorded deferral (ADR-109, "v3.5") — deliberate, so §5.6 does **not** bump severity, but it is a headline parity gap that loses the print-then-cut sale · **P1** for the print-and-cut workflow / P2 laser production · effort **M** (ADR-109's marker detection is the natural foundation; the deferral note says as much).
- **G-S12-2 · 12.2 Overlay accuracy (proof, not feature) · vs XCS (−3), LightBurn (−2)** · [⚠HW], no ADR divergence — the overlay exists and the two 2026-07-10 registration bugs are fixed; what's missing is verified accuracy · closing it is a hardware-verification + perceptual-fixture task, not a build · effort **S–M** (needs a machine + a one-time golden capture to seed an ADR-025 fixture).
- **(register note) 12.1 / 12.3 / 12.5 cross-cutting proof debt** — the calibration solve, the trace-to-bed warp, and the board-capture G92 landing are all code-and-tests proven but **never validated on a real camera or machine** (no camera perceptual fixture exists in the tree). This is one proof-debt item spanning three cells, not three feature gaps; it caps how far any of these ratings can be trusted.
