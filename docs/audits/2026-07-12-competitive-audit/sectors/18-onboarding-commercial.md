### State of play

S18 is the sector where KerfDesk's position is most lopsided: it holds the single clearest win in the entire audit at 18.7, and simultaneously posts the roster's weakest scores on every experiential and ecosystem category. The numbers are driven by the fact that KerfDesk is a young (version 0.1.0), single-maintainer, open-source project competing against products with years of accumulated documentation, community, localization, and asset libraries. Openness is a structural design decision KerfDesk controls; the rest are network effects and content volume that money and time buy, and KerfDesk has spent neither yet.

On **onboarding (18.1)** KerfDesk sits mid-pack at 5. It genuinely has beginner scaffolding — the ADR-092 Device Setup wizard with a readiness checklist and a passive non-nagging nudge, plus the ADR-111 CNC beginner mode with a material picker that auto-fills safe-default feeds. But it deliberately ships no first-run tour, welcome modal, or what's-new dialog (WORKFLOW F-A1), the beginner mode is CNC-only (laser layer cards always expose every field), and prior internal findings flagged CNC beginner-safety default divergences (through-cut default with tabs off and no work-zero advisory). The beginner references — Easel (the purpose-built CNC-beginner tool) and xTool Studio/XCS (EasySet presets, one-click auto-adjust, AI helpers, guided first cut) — sit at 9, and even LightBurn's Find My Laser wizard and MillMage's explicit Beginner Setup mode land at 7. KerfDesk is behind on guidance, not on machine setup.

**Documentation (18.2), learning ecosystem (18.3), and localization (18.6)** are where the gap is widest and hardest to close. LightBurn is the roster benchmark at 10 in both docs and community (a versioned reference plus a single-PDF manual; a ~28.6k-topic forum, YouTube, and huge Facebook groups), with VCarve close behind. KerfDesk's end-user documentation is two markdown guides plus a README, exposed in-app only through three native-alert help entries — and, materially, the guides are referenced by `docs/...` paths that are not shipped in the deployed web bundle (confirmed: `public/` contains no `docs/` folder), so they resolve to 404 for a web user. That is a documentation defect, not merely thin coverage, and pins 18.2 at 3. On community (18.3) KerfDesk scores a token 1 — a README "Help build it" section routing to GitHub issues, no forum, Discord, tutorials, or videos — against a field where even the smallest CNC communities (Easel, Carbide) sit at 6–7. Localization is a clean 0: no i18n framework, no language switcher, English-only literals, against LightBurn's ~25 languages with RTL.

**Assets (18.4)** and **extensibility (18.5)** are more nuanced. On assets KerfDesk's bundled offline Design Library (~76 provenance-tagged entries — though 47 are Lucide icons, leaving ~29 real designs — plus a parametric box generator and material libraries) earns a 4: it beats the CNC senders that ship nothing (MillMage and LaserGRBL at 0), but it is dwarfed by XCS's Atomm ecosystem (1000+ free project files, one-click open, publish-to-community, at 9), Easel's 3M+ shape Pro library (8), and VCarve's Design & Make store (8). On extensibility the whole roster is weak — LightBurn, the laser reference, manages only macros with no plugin API (4) — but KerfDesk is at the bottom with a 1: no plugin system, no scripting, no user-savable macros (the console's quick-commands are a fixed per-driver list), only file-format interop. VCarve's Pro-only LUA Gadgets SDK is the roster's best at 7, and even LaserGRBL's shareable custom-button macros plus GPL forkability (5) outrank us.

The counterweight is **price, licensing & openness (18.7)**, where KerfDesk is the clear roster leader at 10. It is free, MIT-licensed (verified: standard MIT in `LICENSE`, `license: "MIT"` in `package.json`, ADR-120), with a public source repository, a zero-install web PWA plus a free Windows desktop build, and no account, sign-up, or telemetry. Every commercial competitor is paid and closed (LightBurn/MillMage $99+, VCarve $349+, Easel subscription with a crippled 1-export/week free tier, Carbide Core machine-locked). The only other free-and-open product, LaserGRBL, is GPL but dormant — no release in ~16 months — and Windows-only. KerfDesk's combination of $0 + permissive MIT + public source + cross-platform + actively developed + no telemetry is genuinely unmatched here. The one honest caveat is cadence: real release frequency and the self-hosted update feed are not observable from a single pinned snapshot, and the Windows packaged-update path is CLAIMED, not verified.

The net picture for buyers: KerfDesk wins decisively for the cost- and privacy-sensitive, open-source-preferring user, and loses decisively for anyone who values a mature manual, an active community, a large asset library, or a non-English UI. For the two personas this sector weights most (P2 CNC beginner at weight 8, P1 laser hobbyist at 4), the onboarding, docs, and community deficits bite hardest; the openness win, while real, does not offset them in those weightings.
