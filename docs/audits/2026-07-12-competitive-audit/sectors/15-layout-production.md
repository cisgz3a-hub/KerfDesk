### State of play

Layout & production is the sector where KerfDesk's board-centric design philosophy both pays off and shows its ceiling. KerfDesk was built around a single physical *board* — you capture its outline (rect or circle), then fit or tile **one** design into it — and that lineage produces one genuine, defensible lead (stock-shape support, 15.4) and one real convenience the free/consumer tools lack (an actual auto-fill, 15.2). But the same single-design, board-bound framing is exactly what the production category rewards least: the moment a shop needs *different* parts on a sheet, personalized copies, or true material optimization, KerfDesk drops to the floor of the roster. Two of the six categories — true nesting (15.3) and batch/variable content (15.5) — are hard zeros, and one of those zero rows is a category where even a consumer tool (XCS) and a $99 CNC app beat us decisively.

The array picture (15.1) is the clearest "solid but boxed-in" story. KerfDesk's 'Array on board' does a real rows×cols grid with mm spacing and, better, a fit-as-many-as-fit auto-count, all as one undoable, double-burn-guarded edit. That is more than a toy. But it only tiles the one selected design, only inside the captured board, and offers no circular/radial array, no copy-along-path, and no free-canvas design-time step-and-repeat — design-time replication is just in-place Duplicate (`Cmd+D`). LightBurn owns the category outright at 10 (Grid + radial + Virtual synced clones + Copy-Along-Path), with MillMage (its inherited grid+circular engine) and VCarve (Array Copy + Copy-Along-Vectors + linked toolpath array) close behind at 9, and XCS a solid 7 with grid+circular. KerfDesk's 5 sits above only the free tools — it beats LaserGRBL (0, no array at all) and Easel (3, community 'Replicator' app plus manual copy/paste).

Auto-fit / fill-the-board (15.2) is KerfDesk's best-placed *mainstream* capability and the source of a legitimate win over the consumer field. `fitObjectToRegion` scales-and-centers one design into the board rotation-safely (it uses the rotated footprint's AABB so a rotated part cannot overflow the physical board), and 'Fill the board' auto-counts a uniform grid of copies. For the extremely common "fill a sheet with N identical coasters" job this is a one-action, board-aware auto-fill — and XCS, Easel and LaserGRBL have *no* auto-fit engine at all (XCS's batch swaps content but never auto-packs; Easel's 'Tiling' only splits an oversized job). Where KerfDesk loses is against the true packers: LightBurn/MillMage Quick Nest auto-pack multiple *different* parts' bounding boxes (8), and VCarve Pro's True Shape Nesting arranges by actual contour for optimal usage (9). KerfDesk's fill is single-design and grid-only — "maximizes stock for identical copies only," as ADR-125 concedes — so it cannot touch mixed-part stock optimization.

True nesting (15.3) is a roster-wide desert with one oasis, and KerfDesk is in the sand with most of the field. VCarve Pro's True Shape Nesting is the reference (10); LightBurn has a workable-but-clunky true-shape path via a built-in SVGnest.com round-trip (4); and everyone else — KerfDesk, MillMage's stable 0.8.02 (True Nest is 0.9 early-access Pro, not yet on sale), XCS, Easel, LaserGRBL — sits at 0. So while KerfDesk's absence here is a real gap versus VCarve (−10), it is a shared absence, not a place where KerfDesk is uniquely behind the pack. Batch & variable content (15.5) is the opposite and the sector's most damaging hole: KerfDesk is 0 (no variable text, serial, CSV/data-merge, or barcode/QR anywhere; text objects are static), while LightBurn leads at 10 (serial/date/CSV + QR/Barcode), MillMage 8, and even the consumer XCS reaches 7 with per-copy text/image/vector merge. Here KerfDesk is beaten by the entire paid/consumer field and ties only Easel and LaserGRBL at the bottom.

The lone unambiguous win is stock-shape support (15.4), and it is real: KerfDesk is the **only** roster product that models circular stock (a `BoardShape` union of rect + circle, captured by four-corner/typed size or centre + diameter, respected by fit and array), on top of rectangular boards and a CNC job-tiling model for oversized work. Every competitor — LightBurn, MillMage, XCS, Easel, VCarve — is rectangular-workspace-only, and LaserGRBL has merely a rectangular preview with no material model (3). That earns KerfDesk an 8 against a rect-only field at 6. The honest caveats keep it off a 10: nobody in the roster (KerfDesk included) supports arbitrary custom-outline or offcut/remnant stock, and KerfDesk's own circle support is conservative — fit/array only use the inscribed square (`d/√2`), wasting the circular margin. Finally, repeat-job ergonomics (15.6) is a competent middle: KerfDesk has full project save/load (.lf2), a first-party Design Library of editable-vector templates, and a parametric Box Generator (6), but lacks the daily-driver conveniences the rest have — no recent-files list (even LaserGRBL has that), no saved-job/cut-settings preset, no re-run-last-job. VCarve's production-grade Toolpath Templates + Job Setup Sheets + Gadgets lead at 10, with LightBurn/MillMage/XCS clustered at 7.
