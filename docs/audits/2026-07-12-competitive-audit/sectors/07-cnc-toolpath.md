### State of play

This sector splits the roster in half. LightBurn, LaserGRBL, and xTool Studio simply do not compete — all three are laser-side products with no milling operations of any kind — which leaves a five-way contest: KerfDesk, MillMage, Easel, Carbide Create, and VCarve. VCarve V12.5 is the quality ceiling in seven of eight categories (drilling is the exception, where MillMage's four-type drill editor is the widest documented control set), and the pattern against it is the same one S6 showed against LightBurn: everywhere KerfDesk has built a correct core, VCarve has the same core plus a user-control layer — lead-in/out and start points on top of profiling, machining allowance and a separate final pass on top of depth ladders, a per-machine chipload database on top of tool definitions.

KerfDesk's CNC engine is more real than its 0.x-style breadth suggests. Profiling (7.1) does inside/outside/on-path with containment-aware clipper2 offsets, inner-before-outer ordering, and opt-in climb/conventional enforced by shoelace-orientation reversal — that is genuinely more direction control than Easel documents and matches Carbide Create's free tier, short of MillMage's four entry methods and VCarve's leads/last-pass control. Pocketing (7.2) ships two strategies (contour-parallel rings with a wall-finishing last ring, plus ADR-105 Easel-parity raster sweeps) with island handling — more than Easel's single simplified fill, level with Carbide Create, but with no rest machining, no adaptive clearing, and no wall allowance against MillMage's Rest Pocket and VCarve's multi-tool pocketing. Depth management (7.6) has a distinctive safety story — preflight blocks over-deep cuts and then re-proves the floor on the emitted G-code text, something no competitor documents — but lacks the stock-to-leave/finishing-allowance concept every serious CAM here has in some form.

The headline surprise is V-carving (7.4) and 3D relief (7.8): KerfDesk beats MillMage outright in both, because at pinned 0.8.02 MillMage has neither — both are "MillMage Pro" features that exist only in an unreleased 0.9.00 Early Access, a gap its own community flags against VCarve. KerfDesk's V-carve is a true medial-axis ladder with a VCarve-style flat-depth cap and two-stage flat-bit clearing, and it carries the sector's only pipeline-level proof: an analytic perceptual test that a 90° bit carves the exact pyramid frustum through the real compile path. Its relief pipeline (STL both formats → heightmap → waterline roughing leaving 0.5 mm → scallop-driven finishing skim → contained three.js 3D pane) is a free-tier capability that Easel and Carbide Create both paywall. The honest caveats: carved-letter quality has never been compared against VCarve or Carbide Create side by side, the relief output has never been rendered or cut, STL import is reachable only by drag-and-drop (S8-F5, still unfixed), and there is no image-to-heightmap route at all.

The sector's clearest weakness is drilling (7.3). KerfDesk's single behavior — peck cycle at the bounding-box center of every closed shape, feed pinned to plunge — is a "basic" anchored 4: no point drilling, no dwell, no retract configuration, no plain-vs-peck choice, and drilling at the bbox center of arbitrary closed shapes has never been checked against any reference CAM. MillMage documents Plunge/Peck/Spot/Countersink with dwell and retract control; even Easel's locked bit-diameter holes are a more predictable contract. Tool library (7.7) is the other structural gap: KerfDesk's 18 starter bits, custom bits, and a three-tier chipload calculator make a good beginner story (closer to Easel's guidance model than to a CAM database), but tools store no per-tool feeds or flutes, and the chipload chart is explicitly provisional and has never been validated by a real cut — against MillMage's and Carbide Create's per-tool databases and VCarve's per-machine material-specific system, 6 is the ceiling.

Two governing caveats. First, nothing in this sector, on the KerfDesk side, has ever touched a machine — every CNC hardware pass is CLAIMED, so all correctness rests on unit tests and one analytic perceptual fixture; pocket floor coverage in particular is asserted structurally and has never been rendered. Second, per §5.6 the CNC reference for shared workflows is effectively Easel (ADR-105/111/112 are explicit Easel-parity decisions), and one divergence is mis-documented: the tabs-off-by-default-at-full-depth divergence claims to be "recorded in the CNC-defaults ADR," but no such ADR exists in DECISIONS.md — un-ADR'd doc drift that bumps the 7.5 gap's severity.
