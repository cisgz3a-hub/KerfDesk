## ADR-664 - The Falcon A1 Pro's LED is its own laser at low power

**Status:** Accepted by the maintainer on 2026-10-22. Replaces ADR-635; amends ADR-603 and ADR-605. Software verification only; the dot has not yet been lit on a machine by KerfDesk.

**Context.** ADR-635 switched "assist light 0" over the A1 Pro's network link and took it for the red pointer on the head. On the maintainer's machine it lit the wrong light ("its the wrong laser dot. I need the laser dot or LED light in the laser head that shows where it will burn"). That identification was inferred from Creality's code, not measured.

A second look at Falcon Design Space 1.9.2 (`FalconDesignSpace.exe`, its Qt resource QML and string table) found:

- Assist light 0 is switched in one place only: camera calibration turns it off while it photographs the bed and back on afterwards (together with assist light 2 and `/work/laserAssistled` on the Falcon T1 only). No button switches it.
- The control Creality gives for alignment is **Laser on** in the device panel: `deviceController.fire(true, laserFirePower)` with power 1-10, tooltip "You can fire to see the laser spot for precise alignment." It is refused unless the machine is ready and the lid is shut, and it turns itself off after a 30 s countdown.
- A user who built a cross-laser add-on for the A1 Pro (https://makerworld.com/models/2324955) reports the same: the 20 W head is turned on at low power for positioning, and only the 2 W infrared head, whose beam is invisible, has a built-in pointer, which lights only while framing.

So the 20 W head has no separate LED. The dot that shows where it will burn is the laser itself at low power. Asked how LED on should behave, the maintainer chose to copy Creality: the laser at a capped low power, off after 30 s, with a safety warning, and the network request removed. Their machine has the 20 W blue head.

**Decision.**

- `PositioningLight` keeps the G-code mapping. The Falcon kind becomes `{ kind: 'laser-dot', powerPercent, offsetXmm: 0, offsetYmm: 0, afterFrame? }`: power above 0 and at most 5% (Fire's hard cap, ADR-162), default 1%, and no offset, because the dot is the laser spot. It is valid only on the `creality-falcon-a1-pro` command set. A saved ADR-635 light (`creality-falcon-pointer`) is refused, never turned into a laser dot: profile recovery drops it with a note to tick the new box, and a project drops it silently.
- Machine Setup → Positioning LED / crosshair on the A1 Pro shows one tick, **Show where it will burn with the laser at low power**, a warning that this is the cutting laser (safety glasses, keep the power low), the dot power and Centre & LED after Frame. With the infrared module fitted it says the 1064 nm beam is invisible, and LED on refuses.
- **LED on**, **LED off**, **Artwork centre & LED** and Centre & LED after Frame keep the owned controller transaction of ADR-603 and ADR-605: Idle check, beam-off `M5`, fence, beam-off move and position check. Only the light step changes. Before the dot goes on, the transaction takes the Fire latch (`fireActive`), flushes older override flags with a fence, resets the power override to 100% (a realtime byte on its own, as for Fire), then sends Fire's own `G1 F<Frame feed> M3 S<capped S>`. Off is `M5`. The dot also needs a controller and profile approved for low-power Fire, and a laser module that is not reported absent; it does not need Labs or the momentary Fire setting, because ticking the box is the opt-in.
- While the dot is lit it holds the Fire latch, so every existing Fire guard applies: jog, Frame, Start, Home, auto-focus, the Console, overrides and remote control refuse; Disconnect, an Alarm and closing the window put it out; the Live Motion bar says the laser dot is on and offers **LASER OFF**. LED off, LED on and Artwork centre are exempt from the latch they own, because each puts the dot out with an acknowledged `M5` before anything else. The momentary Fire button's window-wide release (pointer up, blur) no longer drops the dot's latch, which that button does not hold.
- The dot goes off by itself 30 s after it was lit, through its own LED off transaction. If another owned exchange holds the controller, it tries again a second later. If that LED off is refused, it sends `M5` the way LASER OFF does. LASER OFF and the momentary Fire release also record the dot off.
- A GRBL error answer to the dot's line proves it never lit, since GRBL checks a whole block before running any of it. A write that may have reached the controller keeps the latch, LASER OFF and the 30 s timer.
- The desktop camera bridge's `/falcon/pointer` route, its client and the Falcon discovery for it are removed.

**Consequences.** The A1 Pro's LED now shows the exact burn spot, works in the web app as well as Desktop, and needs no network link. It emits real laser light: a slow jog is refused while it is lit, so it cannot draw a line, but a dot left on paper at a few percent for 30 s can still mark it. The infrared head has no LED in KerfDesk. Its own pointer only lights while framing.

**Verification.** Unit tests cover the light's validation, normalization and refusal of the retired kind, the infrared refusal, the editor's one tick and power field, and the transaction: `M5` before the dot, the power reset just before it, the capped S at 1% and 5%, the beam-off move before Centre's dot, the Fire latch blocking jog and Frame but not the dot's own controls, the 30 s auto-off and its restart when lit again, a GRBL refusal recording the dot off, a lost write keeping LASER OFF and the timer, and LASER OFF recording it off. Hardware qualification on an A1 Pro remains open.
